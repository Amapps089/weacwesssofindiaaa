04 — BEHIND THE CLOTH

Made slowly.
Meant to last.

Behind every textile is a chain of knowledge — growers, dyers, spinners, pattern-makers and weavers. Techniques are passed from hand to hand, adapting with every generation.

Explore the process with curiosity. Learn the names of the crafts. Support the artisans and communities who carry them forward.

A textile is more than a garment: it can hold clues to geography, trade, local ecology, ceremony and community memory.

05 — BEHIND EVERY THREAD

From fibre
to heirloom.

A handwoven textile is the result of many patient stages. Each community adapts materials, tools and techniques to its own landscape and traditions.

01
✳

PREPARATION

Fibre & yarn

Cotton, silk, wool and other fibres are prepared and spun into yarn, ready for the loom.

02
◉

COLOUR

Dyeing

Yarns or finished cloth are coloured. Some traditions use natural dyes; others use modern dyes.

03
╳

THE LOOM

Warp & weft

Threads cross to form cloth. In ikat, yarn is resist-dyed before weaving to create aligned patterns.

04
❋

DETAIL

Motif & finish

Borders, motifs and finishing touches bring the textile together before it is checked and worn.

A NOTE FOR LEARNERS

More than
a garment.

This educational atlas is an introduction to selected textile traditions across India's 28 states. Some regions are known for sarees; others are represented by shawls, wraps, embroidery and tribal textiles. Names, techniques and meanings can vary by community, maker and place.

Explore with curiosity, credit the artisans, and remember that living craft traditions continue to change with each generation.
